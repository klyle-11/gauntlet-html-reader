Pattern Scale Notes

Two-color halftone

Every shade is a circle’s size.

One indigo navy, one off-white, and a grid of dots that grow. No grays and no transparency: tone comes from how much paper each dot covers.

See the scale

Three ingredients

The whole page is drawn the same way: a fixed grid, a function that says how dark each point should be, and a rule that turns darkness into radius.

01 — GRID

A grid, turned 45°

Dots sit on a square grid rotated the way print screens are, so the eye reads tone instead of rows and columns.

02 — FIELD

A field of darkness

Every point asks a function how dark it should be: a ramp, a sphere, a ripple, a tide. The function is the picture.

03 — THRESHOLD

Darkness becomes radius

Each radius is solved so the dot covers exactly its share of the cell. At about 79% neighbours touch; at 100% the paper is gone.

Tone without a third color.

The sphere at the top is lit from wherever your pointer is; move it and the shading follows. The tide below drifts slowly unless your system asks for reduced motion.

The palette is two values, set once at the top of the stylesheet. Invert swaps them, and every dot is redrawn in the other color.
